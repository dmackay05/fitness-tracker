Banded Push-ups
Chest · Triceps Mini loop (med/heavy)
3–4×10–15sets × reps
▼
Chest to floor, elbows 45° back — push the floor away explosively
Setup
Mini loop band draped across your upper back, ends looped around your thumbs. Get into push-up position — band should add resistance hardest at the top of each rep.
Form Cues
  • Hands slightly wider than shoulder-width, fingers spread wide on the floor.
  • Body one straight line head to heels — squeeze glutes, brace core like you're about to take a punch.

  • Sagging hips — your low back dips because your core is off. Squeeze your glutes and try again.
  • Elbows flaring to 90 degrees — stresses your shoulders. Angle them back.

💡 If full push-ups are too hard right now, start from your knees with the band. You'll progress to full within a few weeks — that's the goal.

Three moves, one round, no rest between — straight through. Your upper body is already loaded, so your core picks up more stabilizing work. Obliques get their dedicated work on Friday with the Woodchop.

A1
Hollow Body Hold
Rectus Abdominis · Full Core Bodyweight
1×20–30shold
▼
Press lower back into floor, ribs down — one rigid curved line
Setup
Lie on your back. Arms overhead, legs straight and lifted about 6 inches off the ground. Press your lower back firmly into the floor — your body forms a shallow dish shape.
Form Cues
  • Lower back stays in contact with the floor — that's the whole exercise. If it lifts, raise your legs higher or bend your knees.
  • Arms reach back, legs reach forward — full-body tension in both directions.
  • Ribs down, not flared. Brace like you're bracing for a punch.
  • Breathe in short controlled breaths — don't hold your breath to maintain position.
  • Build from 20 seconds toward 45 seconds over weeks.
Common Mistakes
  • Lower back arching — raise your legs until the back flattens, then work from there.
  • Holding your breath — keep breathing, even if shallow.
  • Arms drifting forward — they stay overhead to maintain full tension.
💡 This is the foundational ab exercise — it teaches your core to brace under load, not just crunch. Everything else in this circuit builds on this pattern.

Squat
Quads · Glutes Tube 40–50 lb → 70–90 lb stacked
4×12–15sets × reps
▼
Sit back into the chair behind you — chest proud, knees out
Setup
Stand on the band center, feet shoulder-width apart. Hold handles at shoulder height or loop the band so it sits across the back of your neck and shoulders.
Form Cues
  • Push hips back and down like sitting into a chair behind you.
  • Lower until thighs are at least parallel to the floor — deeper if mobility allows.
  • Chest up, back straight — don't let your torso collapse forward.
  • Drive through your whole foot to stand. Don't shift onto your toes.
  • Knees track over your toes — actively push them outward, don't let them cave in.
Common Mistakes
  • Knees caving inward (valgus collapse) — push them out over your pinky toes.
  • Chest dropping forward — keep your torso upright.
  • Partial squats — at least parallel. Half reps leave the best gains behind.
Setup
Feet shoulder-width or slightly wider, toes slightly out. Hold 10 lb dumbbell vertically at chest height, both hands cupping the top end.
Form Cues
  • Push hips back and down, squatting as deep as mobility allows.
  • Keep dumbbell at chest — it acts as a counterbalance to help you stay upright.
  • Drive knees out over toes throughout.
  • Drive through whole foot to stand. Squeeze glutes at top.
  • Keep chest tall and elbows inside knees at the bottom.
Common Mistakes
  • Letting dumbbell drop as you squat — keep it at chest height the whole time.
  • Heels coming up — widen stance slightly if this happens.
  • Not squatting deep — use the weight as a counterbalance to sink lower.
💡 Goblet squat is one of the best teaching tools for squat mechanics — the counterbalance naturally keeps you upright and helps you achieve more depth.
Setup
Place the stability ball between your mid-back and a smooth wall. Feet shoulder-width apart, stepped out about 12–18 inches from the wall — further than feels natural at first. The ball should sit at the curve of your lower back. Stand tall against it before you begin.
Form Cues
  • Bend your knees and lower yourself slowly — the ball rolls up your back as you descend. This is correct.
  • Lower until thighs are parallel to the floor. Your shins should be roughly vertical — knees not shooting past toes.
  • Pause at the bottom for 1–2 seconds. The ball takes the lower back load out of the equation — use this to focus entirely on depth and knee tracking.
  • Drive through your heels to stand. The ball rolls back down as you rise.
  • Knees track over your toes throughout — actively push them outward.
Common Mistakes
  • Feet too close to the wall — your knees will shoot forward. Step your feet further out until your shins stay vertical at the bottom.
  • Ball too high on the back — it should be at mid-back, not upper back or neck.
  • Pushing through toes on the way up — drive through the whole foot, heels especially.
💡 Use this as your warm-up squat before Tuesday and Friday band sessions — 2 sets of 10 with no extra load, just the ball. It grooves the squat pattern perfectly, takes all the balance demand away, and lets you find depth without worrying about form. Your SI joint will thank you — no compression from overhead loading, just guided movement.

02
Romanian Deadlift
Hamstrings Tube 40–50 lb → 90+ lb stacked
3–4×10–12sets × reps
▼
Push hips straight back to the wall — handles glued to your legs
Setup
Stand on the band center, feet hip-width. Handles in front of your thighs, arms straight.
Form Cues
  • Slight knee bend — keep it fixed. This is a hip hinge, not a squat.
  • Push your hips straight back as the handles slide down your legs.
  • Back completely flat — stop at whatever depth your hamstrings limit you, not lower.
  • Drive hips forward to stand, squeezing glutes hard at the top.
  • Handles stay close to your body the whole way — almost brushing your legs.
Common Mistakes
  • Rounding the lower back — the most dangerous mistake. Stop higher if needed.
  • Bending knees too much — turning it into a squat. Fixed, slight knee bend only.
  • Looking up / neck hyperextension — keep head neutral, eyes toward the floor.
Setup
Stand on one foot. Hold 10 lb dumbbell in opposite hand. Slight bend in standing knee. Use 2 lb to learn the balance first.
Form Cues
  • Hinge forward at hip, dumbbell lowering toward floor as free leg extends behind you.
  • Keep back flat and hips square — don't let the hip of the lifted leg rotate open.
  • Lower until strong hamstring stretch in the standing leg.
  • Drive standing hip forward to return upright. Squeeze glute at top.
  • Move slowly — this is as much a balance drill as a strength exercise.
Common Mistakes
  • Rotating hips open to the side — keep them level and facing the floor.
  • Rushing — loss of balance usually means going too fast.
  • Rounding lower back — keep flat even if it means less range.

Banded Glute Bridge

3×15–20sets × reps
▼
Drive through heels, squeeze glutes hard at the top — hold 2 sec
Setup
Lie on your back, knees bent, feet flat on the floor hip-width apart. Tube band across your hips — hold each side down with your hands or loop handles under your feet.

  • Drive through your heels to lift hips — straight line from knees to shoulders at the top.

  • Brace your core first — the ball is unstable, so your stabilizers fire from rep one.

Supported Low Lunge (Hip Flexor Release)

Setup
Come into a low lunge — one knee on the mat, front foot forward. Place one yoga block under each hand at the lowest height to start, inside your front foot. Back knee can have a folded mat or towel under it for comfort.
What You're Doing
  • Sink your hips straight down toward the floor — don't push forward. Gravity does the work.
  • Let your hands rest on the blocks rather than gripping — this signals your nervous system to release, not brace.
  • As the stretch softens after 30–40 seconds, raise the blocks to a higher setting for more depth, or lower them for less.

💡 Your hip flexors are under constant compression during cycling — they shorten every pedal stroke. This undoes that. It's one of the highest-value stretches in the whole program for what you're doing week to week.
S2
Block-Supported Pigeon Pose (Glute + Hip Opener)
Yin · 90 sec/sideGlutes · Piriformis · Hip Rotators
3 mintotal
▼
Setup
From low lunge, swing your front leg across so the shin is roughly parallel to the front of the mat (or as close as your hip allows). Place one block under the hip of your front leg — this is the key. Without the block, most people collapse to one side and the stretch never reaches the target. The block levels your pelvis and lets the glute actually open.
What You're Doing
  • Both hip bones should feel even — the block under the front hip achieves this if you're less flexible on one side.

Turkish Get-Up
Full Body · Core · Shoulder Stability 8 lb kettlebell
2×3/sidereps · slow
▼

Setup
Lie on your back. Hold the kettlebell in your right hand, arm extended straight up toward the ceiling. Right knee bent, foot flat on the floor. Left arm and left leg extended at 45 degrees from your body. Eyes stay on the kettlebell the entire movement — this is your anchor point.

  • Step 1 — Roll to elbow: Press through your left elbow and right foot to roll up onto your left forearm. KB still pointing straight up.

  • Step 3 — Hip bridge: Drive your right foot into the floor and lift your hips up into a bridge. Body forms a straight line from knee to shoulder.

  • Reverse every step exactly. Step back into the lunge, sweep the leg forward, lower to the hip bridge, sit to the hand, lower to the elbow, roll to your back.

  • Complete all reps on one side before switching. That's one set.

  • Rushing through the steps — each transition should be deliberate and stable before moving to the next.
  • Bent elbow on the supporting arm — the locked arm is a structural pillar. Keep it straight.

🩹 Lower Back Flare-Up

Three things cause most lower back pain: tight hip flexors pulling the pelvis forward, inactive glutes offloading onto the lower back, and an undertrained core leaving the spine unsupported. Don't stop moving — that makes it worse.

Modify These

ExerciseStatusFix
Romanian DeadliftReduce loadStop the hinge before any back pull
Bulgarian Split SquatAvoid if painfulSwap for regular banded squat
Jump Rope2–3 days offResume when pain reduces
Bike RidesEasy pace onlyResume intensity gradually

What To Do Daily During Flare-Up

🌅 Morning — Before Anything Else
1. Supine Knee Hugs — 90 sec
Lie on your back, pull both knees to your chest, rock gently side to side. One slow rock per breath. This decompresses the SI joint and lower back after sleep compression.

2. Cat-Cow — 10 slow reps
Hands and knees. Inhale into the arch (belly drops, chest lifts). Exhale into the round (back arches up, chin tucks). 4–6 seconds per rep. Lubricates every spinal segment.

3. 90/90 Hip Flexor Stretch — 60 sec each side
Kneel on one knee, front shin vertical, hips square. Shift hips gently forward until you feel the front of the back hip/groin. Squeeze the back glute lightly. 60 seconds each side.

4. Child's Pose — 90 sec
From kneeling, sit back toward heels, arms stretched forward, forehead down. Wide knees gives more hip space. Breathe into your back body — feel your lower back decompress.
⚡ Pre-Workout — Before Any Training Session
1. Stability Ball Glute Bridge — 2 × 10 reps (no band)
Upper back on the stability ball, feet flat on the floor hip-width apart. Drive through heels to lift hips — squeeze glutes hard at the top for 2 seconds, lower slowly. No band — this is activation only. Your glutes need to be firing before any load goes on the spine.

2. Dead Bug — 2 × 6/side (shortened range)
Lie on back, lower back pressed completely flat to the floor. Extend one arm and opposite leg — only as far as the back stays flat (often just 30–45°). Exhale as you extend. If your back lifts even slightly, shorten the range. No popping. Skip if it causes pain today.
🌙 Evening — Priority Poses for Back Relief
1. Dragon Pose (Low Lunge) — 3–5 min each side
Front foot forward, back knee down on a folded blanket. Sink hips low and hold passively. Let gravity do the work — this releases the hip flexors that are pulling your pelvis forward and compressing your lower back. The first 90 seconds feels intense. Stay.

2. Sleeping Swan (Yin Pigeon) — 3–5 min each side
From all fours, bring one knee forward behind your wrist, extend back leg straight, fold forward. Use a pillow under the front hip if needed. You'll feel it deep in the outer glute — that's the piriformis releasing, which directly decompresses the SI joint.

3. Supine Twist — 2–3 min each side
Lie on back, bring one knee across your body toward the floor on the opposite side. Arms wide, palms up. Don't force the knee down — let it rest. Gaze away from the bent knee. This directly decompresses the SI joint and lower back.

4. Legs Up the Wall — 5–10 min
Hips close to wall, legs straight up, arms out palms up. Total surrender. Decompresses the spine and activates the parasympathetic nervous system — where recovery actually begins.
🔥 Heat, Not Ice 15–20 min of heat on the lower back morning and/or evening. Heating pad, warm towel, or hot shower. Ice is for acute injury with swelling — this is muscular tightness. Heat is the right tool.
💡 When standing up from a chair: pause 2 seconds, roll your pelvis slightly forward (small arch), then stand. Eliminates most sharp spikes within a few days.

See Your Provider If:

  • Pain is sharp rather than dull and achy
  • Pain radiates into your glutes, hamstrings, or legs
  • Numbness or tingling in legs or feet
  • No meaningful improvement within 1–2 weeks
💪 Golfer's Elbow — Right Arm

Tendinopathy of the flexor-pronator tendons at the medial epicondyle (inside of elbow). Caused by repetitive gripping — bike handlebars, band handles, jump rope. The compression sleeve reduces load at the attachment point by distributing force along the tendon belly.

⚠ Modify These

Bicep / Hammer CurlPause or reduceDrop to 10 lb band, stop if pain flares
Bent-Over RowReduce loadDrop one band level
Jump RopeShorten2–3 min max
KB HalosPauseSkip KB warm-up portion
Bike RidesWear sleevePadded gloves reduce vibration

Your Daily Elbow Routine

🌅 Morning — First Thing (5 min)
Eccentric Wrist Flexion Curl — 3 sets × 15 reps, 30s rest
Sit with right forearm resting on your thigh, palm up, wrist hanging off the edge of your knee. Use your LEFT hand to curl the 2 lb dumbbell up — right hand just holds passively. Now lower with the RIGHT hand only, taking 3–4 seconds to go all the way down. That slow lowering is the entire point. Left hand brings it back up. Repeat.

You should feel a mild working sensation in the forearm — not sharp pain. If it's sharp, use less range of motion, not a lighter weight (you're already at 2 lbs). Do this every single morning including rest days.
🚴 After Rides + After Band Sessions (2 min)
Wrist Flexor Stretch — 30 sec hold × 2
Extend your right arm straight in front of you, palm facing up. Use your left hand to gently pull your right fingers back toward you until you feel a stretch along the inside of your forearm. Hold 30 seconds. Release. Repeat once more. Never force it — the tendon is already irritated.
🌙 Evening (7 min)
Step 1 — Heat (5 min)
Warm compress or heating pad on the inside of the right elbow. 5 minutes. This increases blood flow to the tendon before the massage.

Step 2 — Cross-Friction Massage (60–90 sec)
Use your left thumb to find the bony bump on the inside of your right elbow — that's the medial epicondyle. Apply gentle pressure directly on it and make small slow circles. You should feel a dull ache, not sharp pain. 60–90 seconds.

Step 3 — Forearm Stroke (30 sec)
From that same bump, use your left thumb to stroke slowly down the inside of your forearm toward your wrist — firm, slow strokes following the tendon. 10–12 strokes.

Step 4 — Wrist Flexor Stretch (30 sec × 2)
Same as after rides. Finish every evening session with this.

🦴 SI Joint + Core Bracing

The SI joint sits where your lower spine meets your pelvis. When your core doesn't brace before a rep, your pelvis rocks slightly with each movement — the SI joint absorbs that. Bracing before every rep is the fix. Consistent bracing + daily yoga is the long-term solution.

B1
The Brace — Master This First
3 sec · Before every repCore · Pelvis · SI Joint
3 secpre-rep
▼
How to find it: Put one hand on your belly. Breathe in — then instead of sucking in, push your belly outward like you're bracing for a punch. It should firm up and press out. That pressurization is intra-abdominal pressure — it stabilizes your spine before any load hits it.

Pre-rep ritual: Breathe in → brace outward → lock ribcage down → then move. Every single rep. Think of your core as a pressurized soda can. Pressurized = rigid and strong. Open = crushes easily.
  • Sucking the belly in — that reduces stability. Push out.
  • Only bracing on heavy sets — the SI joint gets irritated from volume, not just load.
B2
Dead Bug — SI-Safe Technique
High SI risk if done wrongCore · Pelvis · Anti-Extension
Fulltechnique
▼
Big exhale first — flattens the lower back. Slide your hand under your lower back: zero gap before you start. Brace outward. Extend a leg only as far as the lower back stays flat — often just 30–45° at first. Exhale as you extend. The popping you feel when the back lifts = the SI joint being torqued. Shorten the range until it's silent.
  • Back lifting off the floor — shorten range immediately.
  • Moving too fast — slow is the whole stimulus.
